1 of 8

Improving accessibility.

Most apps assume the user reads comfortably. Many users in rural Bangladesh don't, so a screen like this tells them very little.

2 of 8

Three tiers of Adaptive UI

A mascot greets first-time members and asks how comfortable they are with apps like this. Their answer sets a starting tier, and the app quietly nudges them up after a run of easy sessions, or back down after repeated trouble.

3 of 8

One field, one screen, one step at a time.

Mobile number, then a one-time code, then a PIN — three separate screens rather than one crowded form, so nobody loses track of what they've already entered.

4 of 8

A one-time code confirms the number.

Same big keypad, same masked-dot pattern as the PIN screen that follows — one visual language carried through the whole login.

5 of 8

A PIN that is never spoken and is always private.

Each digit is confirmed with a filling dot and a short vibration.

6 of 8

Four choices, stacked, not gridded.

A first-time member sees loan, savings, special savings, and insurance as one simple list — a big icon, a label, and its own listen button on each, colour-coded but never colour alone.

7 of 8

One fact at a time.

Account details page through a single field, with arrows and dots to move between them — nothing to scroll past, nothing to misread.

All figures on these screens are sample data.

8 of 8

Same pattern, every account.

Special savings (DPS) uses the identical paged layout, just in its own teal — one consistent design followed through loan, savings, DPS, and insurance alike.

1 of 5

The orb shows what the assistant is doing.

It has four states: idle, listening, thinking, and speaking. A member can follow along without reading a single word.

2 of 5

A member asks, in Bengali.

Their voice streams live over a WebSocket to a FastAPI server. The ring around the orb moves with their voice, so they know they're being heard.

3 of 5

It checks BRAC's documents first.

Before answering, the system retrieves the most relevant passages from the knowledge base. Answers come from approved material rather than the model's guesswork.

4 of 5

The answer comes back as speech.

The Gemini Live API turns the retrieved information into a natural spoken reply in Bengali, streamed back in real time.

5 of 5

Voice in, grounded answer out.

That pipeline is the whole system, from microphone to speaker. The recorded demo shows it running end to end.

1 of 5

A five-minute interview instead of a form.

Six fixed questions gather what the AI needs to draft the first document. Any team member can revise or skip an answer, right up until it's locked in.

2 of 5

One document at a time, each building on the last.

Charter, then BRD, then SRS, then WBS — drafted in order by a local AI model, each using everything approved so far.

3 of 5

Nothing ships without two signatures.

A Tier 1 and a Tier 2 approver each sign with their own digital signature before a document locks and work can move on.

4 of 5

Every version survives.

Edits, AI rewrites, even reopening an approved document with Restart — nothing is ever deleted. The full history stays one click away.

5 of 5

The team sees the same thing, live.

Comments on the whole project or on one document, plus a live status so nobody double-triggers a generation or overwrites a teammate's edit.
