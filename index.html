Chapter 1 Deep dive · 1 of 3

Agami, rebuilt for members who read little or not at all.

An accessible, adaptive version of BRAC's Agami microfinance app. It leans on sound, pictures and numbers instead of text, adjusts itself to each member's experience, and lets anyone check that BRAC really received the money they handed over.

Live at accessible-agami.onrender.com Four adaptive tiers Bengali audio on every screen Android APK + website, one codebase Built on published research
01 / 10 · The gap

Most banking apps assume the user reads comfortably.

Agami's screens are dense, English-first and full of small text. Many of the members it serves in rural Bangladesh can't use a screen like this without help.

77%of rural households have no or low digital skills
32.5%of mobile-money users have shared their secret PIN with an agent or relative
Sources: BIGD (2023); Chowdhury et al. (2026)
02 / 10 · Grounded in research

Designed from the research on low-literate users.

Before building, I reviewed the literature on interfaces for low-literate users. Its main guidelines shaped every screen:

  • Hear it as well as see it: audio alongside every label
  • Numbers over words: most users read digits even when they can't read text
  • One step at a time: a straight path instead of deep menus
  • Confirm before anything irreversible

Login is a big numeric keypad with a voice guide, one field per screen.

03 / 10 · Four ways to use it

One app, four tiers, matched to the member.

  • ১২৩: a spoken, numbered menu for people who can't read
  • নতুন: guided, one thing at a time
  • মাঝারি: the standard screens, each button with a small 🔊
  • দক্ষ: everything at once, for experienced users

A new member picks a starting level, and the app suggests changes from there.

04 / 10 · The ১২৩ tier

Listen, then press a number.

The ১২৩ menu reads itself aloud, and each number lights up while its line plays. Pressing ০ repeats the menu.

In the two guided tiers, the first tap on any button says what it does and the second tap does it, so nothing happens by accident.

Balances are never read aloud automatically, so people nearby don't hear them unless the member asks.

05 / 10 · Adaptive, but the member decides

It notices how tasks go, and makes an offer.

Each everyday task is scored as clean or rough. Signs of difficulty include tapping ? twice, abandoning a task, three keypad corrections, going round in loops, or replaying the same audio.

  • 8 clean of the last 10, over 3 different days: offer the next tier up
  • 6 rough: offer the tier below
  • Offers appear only on the dashboard or a success page, and declining one pauses them
There is no clock. In the field, a slow task usually means an interruption, not a lack of skill.
06 / 10 · Amounts as pictures

Money you can see.

In the guided tiers every amount is also drawn as the taka notes that make it up, even while the member is typing. Someone who can't read ৳২৮,৫০০ can still recognise twenty-eight purple ১০০০ notes and one green ৫০০.

07 / 10 · Trust

"Did BRAC actually get my money?"

Members often hand repayments to a field worker. Now they can check, without reading, that BRAC recorded it:

  • Every account page leads with the latest payment: ✅ green when BRAC received it, ❌ red for a month with nothing received
  • Every history row says whether BRAC received it, and how (cash, bKash, branch)
  • "Paid, but it's not here?" calls BRAC's own call centre, not the field worker's phone
08 / 10 · Safe by default

Mistakes are hard to make.

  • Hold to confirm: applying for a loan needs a one-second press, so a stray tap can't do it
  • Nothing scrolls: every screen and dialog fits the phone it opens on
  • Shared phones: each member's settings are saved under their own mobile number
  • Text size, high contrast, vibration and auto-read are all in Settings
09 / 10 · Pictures from village life

Icons a member recognises.

Generic app icons mean little to a first-time user, so the icons show village life: a hand with bangles receiving taka for a loan, a clay money bank for savings, a tin-roof house for home, a woman in a saree for the profile, and a passbook for history.

10 / 10 · Try it

One codebase, shipped two ways.

  • Android APK: a wrapper that gives the app the phone's Bengali voice, plays audio without a first tap, and makes Back close dialogs instead of quitting
  • Website: the same files, hosted on Render
  • Research panel: tap the Settings title 7 times to export a log of every task, offer and answer as CSV, for a field study

Chapter 2 Deep dive · 2 of 3

Ask out loud in Bengali. Hear an answer from BRAC's own documents.

A real-time voice assistant built on Google's Gemini Live API, with retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). It answers only from documents BRAC approves, says so when it doesn't know, and runs free on the open internet, including on phones.

Live at brac-voice-assistant.onrender.com Gemini Live · gemini-3.8-live gemini-embedding-001 FastAPI + WebSocket Hybrid RAG Admin panel + document upload
01 / 09 · The interface

One button. Talk, don't type.

Press কথা বলুন ("speak") and ask. The orb shows what the assistant is doing, so the member can follow along without reading:

  • Listening: a smooth wave drawn inward with your voice
  • Searching: it looks through the documents
  • Speaking: bars pulse outward with its voice

Typed questions work too, and the conversation can be shown as a transcript.

02 / 09 · Architecture

The path of one question.

The browser records the microphone at 16 kHz and streams it over a WebSocket to a FastAPI server, which relays it to the Gemini Live API. Gemini's spoken reply streams back the same way and plays as it arrives, at 24 kHz.

Because it streams in both directions, the assistant starts speaking without waiting for a whole answer to be written first.

03 / 09 · Grounding

It has to look things up first.

Gemini gets exactly one tool: search_knowledge_base. Its instructions begin:

"You have no knowledge of your own. Every factual statement you make must come from a passage returned by the search tool."

Before any question about policies, amounts, eligibility or contacts it must call the tool, then answer only from what comes back.

04 / 09 · Retrieval

Hybrid search, tuned for Bengali.

Documents are split by heading into passages of about 900 characters. Each question is scored against every passage in two ways:

  • Meaning: Gemini embeddings find passages that say the same thing in different words
  • Words: word and character-trigram matching catch exact names and terms. Trigrams cope with Bengali word endings: ঋণ, ঋণের, ঋণগুলো

Passages below a minimum score are dropped, and the best four go to Gemini.

05 / 09 · Honesty

When it doesn't know, it says so.

If nothing relevant comes back, it doesn't guess:

দুঃখিত, এই বিষয়ে আমার কাছে তথ্য নেই।

"Sorry, I don't have information on this." If a question is only partly covered, it answers that part and says which part it doesn't have. Greetings and small talk skip the search entirely.

06 / 09 · No code needed

Admins run it from a panel.

  • Choose from 8 Gemini voices, switch the voice model, and set how strict the search is and how many passages are sent
  • Upload a PDF, photo or Word file: Gemini rewrites it as clean text and the knowledge base re-indexes itself
  • Change the Gemini API key from the panel

Settings apply from the next conversation: a live voice session's setup is fixed when it connects.

07 / 09 · Deployment

From my laptop to the open internet, for free.

Phones only allow the microphone over HTTPS, and the audio needs a long-lived WebSocket. Render's free tier provides both. To get there:

  • One server on one port; secrets in environment variables
  • Signed login cookies that survive restarts, and an invite code so strangers can't use up the Gemini quota
  • An optional free Postgres database (Neon) to keep uploads and accounts
  • A phone layout: talk button at the bottom, orb above, screen kept awake
08 / 09 · Field testing

Three problems found in real use, and fixed.

It worked perfectly on the first run after deployment, then got unreliable. Each problem was reproduced and measured before it was fixed.

09 / 09 · Try it

Light enough for free hosting.

The server itself does little work: Gemini handles the speech and the language. Most of the delay is the network round trip to Google.

The free server sleeps after 15 quiet minutes, and the first visit then takes about a minute. This page woke it when it opened; the dot at the top right shows its status.

Problem 1

It stuttered, stopping and starting mid-sentence

Cause, measuredIt was hearing itself. Just 0.15 s of its own voice reaching the mic made Gemini cut its reply within 0.3 s.
Fix: the mic stays muted from the first sound of a reply until the speaker is quiet, and replies are buffered 0.2 s so small gaps don't reopen it.
Problem 2

"Connecting…" took far too long

Cause, measuredEvery conversation rebuilt the Gemini client: about 1.7 s of CPU, roughly 17 s on Render's tenth of a CPU.
Fix: build the client once and reuse it, warmed up in the background at startup.
Problem 3

Stuck on শুনছি…, while typing still worked

Cause, measuredGoogle's preview voice model was failing: gemini-3.1-flash-live-preview failed 5 of 5 voice tests; gemini-3.8-live passed 5 of 5.
Fix: default to the working model, add a model switch to the admin panel, and show a clear "Gemini isn't responding" message.

Chapter 3 Deep dive · 3 of 3

The same conversation, with no cloud at all.

A fully local, real-time Bengali voice assistant. Speech recognition, the language model, retrieval and speech synthesis all run on one machine, so no audio and no document ever leaves it. The design target is under one second from the end of a question to the first word of the answer.

Target: NVIDIA DGX Spark Python asyncio pipeline Silero VAD BanglaASR Ollama · gemma4:26b bge-m3 EMTIAZZ VITS (Bangladeshi voice)
github.com/ayazelahimullick12-droid/Local-RAG-Based-Voice-Assistant ↗
01 / 09 · Why local

Private, free to run, and independent of the internet.

  • Privacy: member questions and BRAC documents never leave the machine
  • No per-use cost: no API bill however much it's used
  • Works offline, where connectivity is poor
  • Latency under our control, with no network round trip

The trade-off: we carry the hardware, and the quality of open models. It's built for the MSI EdgeXpert (NVIDIA DGX Spark: GB10 Grace Blackwell, ARM64, 128 GB unified memory).

02 / 09 · The pipeline

Nine stages, one machine.

A detector notices when you stop talking. Your speech is transcribed, matched against the documents, answered by a local language model, and spoken back phrase by phrase.

The microphone loop never waits for an answer: responses run in the background, so it keeps listening while the assistant talks.

03 / 09 · Models

Open models, chosen for Bengali.

Every model is pre-trained and openly licensed; nothing was trained or fine-tuned. The work is the integration: the real-time pipeline, turn-taking, interruption, Bengali text handling, retrieval and instrumentation.

Speech recognition and speech synthesis each have two fallbacks behind one interface, so switching is a one-line config change.

04 / 09 · Speed

It starts speaking before the answer is finished.

The language model streams its answer word by word. As soon as the first phrase is complete (split on । ? ! , ;, at least 15 characters) it goes to speech synthesis.

While phrase one plays, phrase two is being synthesised and the model is still writing phrase three. Heavy GPU work runs off the main loop, so listening never stalls.

05 / 09 · Every millisecond

Small choices that add up.

  • No hidden "thinking" from the model, and short answers: 120 tokens at most, 40 for greetings
  • Greetings skip the document search entirely
  • A mixture-of-experts model: only 3.8B of its 25.2B parameters work on each word
  • Every model warms up at startup, and the language model is never unloaded
  • Fast, greedy speech decoding; voice detection on the CPU; embeddings cached on disk
06 / 09 · Conversation

It behaves like a conversation, not a command line.

  • Interruptions: start talking over it and it stops at once, drops what it was about to say, and listens
  • Fillers: if no answer has started within 700 ms, a pre-recorded phrase like "একটু দেখি…" plays, never twice in a row
  • Spoken style: no lists, symbols or emoji, because everything is read aloud
  • Memory: the last six messages go with each question
07 / 09 · Bengali text

Numbers read the way a person says them.

Speech models stumble over digits and symbols. Before synthesis, amounts, percentages, dates and decimals, in Bengali or Latin digits, are rewritten as Bengali words.

The example on the right is real output from the project's normaliser.

08 / 09 · Two approaches

Cloud or local: what each one buys you.

The two assistants answer the same kind of question in the same orb interface, but make opposite trade-offs. The cloud version is easy to host and sounds more natural; the local version keeps everything in-house.

09 / 09 · Status

Where it stands, honestly.

The pipeline runs on my own PC. Running it on the DGX Spark is a separate deployment path, prepared with NVIDIA containers but not yet run on the device.

Next: echo cancellation so it works on speakers, streaming speech recognition and synthesis, and a proper accuracy evaluation.

From a six-question interview to signed-off project documents.

A multi-agent project management platform: AI agents draft a project's documents one after another, and the team reviews, signs and versions each one. It runs entirely on a local model, with no API cost.

Each document builds on everything approved before it.

A Q&A agent asks six fixed questions. Then the Charter, Business Requirements (BRD), Software Requirements (SRS) and Work Breakdown Structure (WBS) agents each draft their document in order, from the answers and every document already approved.

One AI call per document keeps it fast on modest hardware.

  • Comments on the whole project or on one document
  • Export to PDF

Chapter 5 A glance

Where field visits happen, how they went, and what still needs fixing.

A Field Visit Tracker for BRAC Microfinance. Staff log their visits, members and staff rate them, managers see it all on a map of Bangladesh, and problems raised during a visit are tracked until someone fixes them.

Live at field-visit-v1-8.onrender.com Interactive drill-down map Member and staff ratings Complaints management Four roles
01 / 04 · The map

Every visit, on a map of Bangladesh.

Each visit records where it happened, star ratings from the member and from staff, notes and quick tags. The map shades each division by how many visits it has had; grey means no data yet.

02 / 04 · Drill down

From the whole country down to one branch.

Select a division and the map zooms in, re-shading each district by its visits. Keep going through BRAC's own structure:

Division → District → Region → Area → Branch

The analytics follow the same selection: total visits, this month's visits, average member and staff ratings, and how many raised issues have been resolved.

03 / 04 · Follow-through

Problems found on a visit don't get lost.

Issues go to a Complaints Management Center. An admin assigns each one to a department's fixer with a deadline; reminder emails go out, overdue items escalate, extensions are recorded, and an audit trail keeps every action.

Staff sign in with their employee PIN or email.
